# Philosophy

## Why This Exists

The Indian Students Protest Vault exists because mainstream narratives often fail to capture the ground reality of student-led democratic movements in India. When thousands of students across the country take to the streets — from Delhi's Jantar Mantar to the smallest town squares — their stories are told in reels, not headlines.

This project is an archival response to a simple question: _"Who will remember?"_

## Motive

**To preserve publicly shared protest media in an organised, searchable, and non-partisan archive before it disappears — whether through algorithmic drift, platform decay, or collective forgetting.**

Instagram reels are ephemeral by design. They surface for 48 hours and then sink into an algorithmic abyss. The Vault pulls them into the light, organises them by place and theme, and makes them discoverable for researchers, journalists, historians, and the public.

This is not activism. It is **witnessing** — the technical act of keeping a record so that the record exists.

## Scope

### In Scope

- **Indian student protests only.** The archive is strictly limited to videos documenting peaceful student-led protests, demonstrations, and related civic actions within India.
- **Publicly available Instagram reels.** Every video in the archive was uploaded to a public Instagram account. We do not host, download, or redistribute media files.
- **Non-partisan curation.** Videos are categorised by event type (protest marches, police conduct, human rights, etc.) and geographic location — not by political alignment.
- **Metadata preservation.** City, state, category, tags, and hashtags are curated to make each video findable through search and filter.

### Out of Scope

- **No editorialising.** The Vault does not write analysis, pick sides, or rank importance. It organises and surfaces.
- **No media hosting.** All embeds point back to Instagram. The Vault is a _directory of public reels_, not a video platform.
- **No political affiliation.** The project has no relationship with any political party, government body, or activist organisation.
- **No user accounts.** Visitors browse anonymously. No login, no tracking, no profiles.
- **No AI generation.** Everything in the archive was created by human participants on the ground.

## Design Ethos

The brutalist aesthetic — heavy borders, hard shadows, bold type — is deliberate. It echoes the rawness of protest footage: unpolished, direct, honest. The Indian flag colour palette (saffron, navy, white, green) anchors the archive to its subject without ornament.

The site should feel like a filing cabinet, not a magazine. It prioritises **findability over flourish**.

## Data Ethos

- **Attribution is preserved.** Every video links back to the original Instagram post.
- **Removal is respected.** Video owners can request removal via Instagram DM. Requests are reviewed within 48 hours.
- **No personal data is collected.** The submission form asks for nothing beyond the Instagram URL and optional metadata tags.
- **The archive is auditable.** The code is open source (MIT). The data flows are transparent.

## Long-term View

Archives are not built in a day. This project grows one submission at a time. The goal is not completeness — it is **presence**. A century from now, someone should be able to look at this collection and understand what happened, where, and when.

That is the vault.
